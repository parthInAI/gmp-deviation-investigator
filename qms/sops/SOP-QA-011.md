> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. Fictional site (Ardmore Biologics). Not a real procedure.

## 1 Purpose
To find the root cause of deviations and choose corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) that stop them happening again.

## 2 Scope
All major and critical deviations, and minor deviations that recur.

## 3 Responsibilities
3.1 The investigator gathers evidence and proposes root cause and CAPA.
3.2 QA approves the root cause, the CAPA and the effectiveness check.

## 4 Procedure
4.1 Start from the evidence, not from a suspected cause. Establish the timeline: which signal moved first, when, and by how much against the normal batch profile.
4.2 List candidate causes and rank them. For each, state the evidence for and against. A cause with no supporting evidence is not ranked.
4.3 Distinguish a process change from a measurement error. When a measured value moves, confirm with an independent measurement (second probe, offline sample, related signals) before concluding the process itself changed.
4.4 Human error may only be concluded after equipment, procedure and system causes have been examined and excluded, and the reasons are documented.
4.5 If the true root cause cannot be confirmed, the report states the most likely root cause, why, and what would confirm it.
4.6 Similar past deviations are checked for recurrence. A recurrence means the earlier CAPA was not effective and must be reviewed.
4.7 Each CAPA has an owner, a due date and an effectiveness check with a measurable criterion (for example: no recurrence in the next 10 batches).
4.8 Every statement in the investigation report must reference its evidence: a data window, a record, a procedure section or a past deviation number.

## 5 Records
Investigation report; CAPA records; effectiveness check results.
